# OTHERSELF — episode 01: The last light, version 2

A small vulnerable observer borrows the authority of station workers to reach the last coastal shuttle. The station is a connected vertical space: ground service channels, galleries at 2.3 metres and upper maintenance decks at 4.8 metres. Each hall combines traversal with stealth. There is no combat, countdown, account or server.

Nim can walk, jump, crawl manually and climb ladders. Workers can operate their role's machinery and climb ladders, but cannot fit through Nim's ducts or jump gaps. The keeper's reader opens a gate; the technician's console operates a bridge or moving cart. Vertical position matters for interactions, vision, possession, collision and sound investigation. Floor pits, gaps in elevated walkways and overhead ducts are real world geometry. See [LEVEL_DESIGN.md](LEVEL_DESIGN.md) for the authored paths and recovery routes.

Nim carries one recovered metal object. Throwing follows a ballistic arc; the impact makes a temporary audible event that causes guards to turn, investigate and search before resuming patrol. A thrown item can be recovered. The preview and physical projectile use the same engine trajectory. A distraction opens a route but does not make Nim invisible. His abandoned body remains vulnerable while possessing a worker.

Possession checks facing, distance and line of sight throughout the 0.6-second connection. Workers recover after release. Physics keeps running for the abandoned body. Mechanisms and workers in visited rooms continue updating; checkpoints, retry and full local saves preserve actual state. Version 1 saves migrate to an entrance compatible with the new geometry.

Art direction: oxidized teal steel, damp concrete, warm copper tread edges, amber practical lighting, plants behind glass and deep structural silhouettes. The four halls share a station vocabulary with distinct lighting and industrial functions. Local albedo, normal and roughness maps provide surface detail; walkable edges and ladders remain legible against background architecture. Rendering uses true 3D geometry with a side camera that follows both horizontal and vertical movement.

Controls use physical keys in all locales: A/D or left/right to move, Space to jump, C/Control to toggle crawl, W/S or up/down for ladders, F to pick up/throw, E to interact, Q to possess/return. Touch controls permit simultaneous movement and actions. Help and contextual prompts use complete Russian, English and Hebrew dictionaries, with RTL text and stable physical control placement.

The deterministic engine has no browser globals. `step(state, dt, {move, climb})` advances a bounded fixed timestep; discrete actions are exported separately. Shared `ROOMS` metadata defines platforms, ladder endpoints, pits, overhead ducts, blockers, height-specific cover, fixtures, patrols and item spawn points. Renderer geometry derives from those collision definitions. State records x/y, vertical velocity, grounded/crouch/ladder status, held items, projectiles and guard search state. Production excludes the development-only state inspection hook.

Resources use local assets, shared geometry/materials and bounded pixel ratios; high and low graphic settings remain available. Desktop/mobile frame-rate targets require actual hardware validation. Pausing, hidden-tab suspension, reduced motion, mute and separate effect/music levels remain supported. Two optional rescued sail creatures produce three endings; the staged finale can be paused or skipped.
